# Brand Key — Decide One

> **Standing:** the positioning frame. `BRAND_HOUSE.md` is the structure,
> `BRAND_BOOK.md` is the voice. **`VISION.md` §11 wins any conflict with this.**
>
> Nine fields, filled from evidence in the repository rather than invented. Where
> something is an assumption rather than a finding, it says so.

---

## 1. Root strengths — what is already true

- **The methods predate the software industry.** Ivy Lee, 1918. The
  urgency/importance split, 1954. Neither needs defending and neither can be
  copied away, because they were never owned.
- **Enforcement rather than suggestion.** Ivy Lee's second line will not open
  until the first is closed. Almost every competitor draws a framework; this one
  makes it binding.
- **A clock, not a checkbox.** Time is attached to the priority it constrains,
  so overcommitment is discovered while planning rather than at midnight.
- **Nothing leaves the device — architecturally.** There is no server to change
  its mind later. Most privacy claims are policies; this one is a missing
  component.
- **Free, and it costs nothing to run.** Zero marginal cost per person.
- **One person builds it**, which is why the feature list can credibly stop.

## 2. Competitive environment

| Who | What they sell | Where they are vulnerable |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Todoist, Things, TickTick | Capture and organise everything | Capture is the problem, not the cure. A full list is the condition being treated |
| Notion, Obsidian | Build your own system | Requires building a system before doing any work |
| Day One, Journey | A journal with sync and media | Sync means a server, which means an account and a subscription |
| Sunsama, Motion | Planning with AI assistance | Decides *for* you; also $20–34/month |
| Paper and a pen | Nothing. Costs ₹1,500 a year | No enforcement, no timing, no search |

**The real competitor is not an app. It is a person opening a blank list and
writing eleven things on it.**

## 3. Target

**Primary:** someone who already knows what productivity advice says and still
ends most days having done the wrong things. They do not need motivating; they
need the choice made smaller and then held.

Not a beginner, not a system-builder, not someone looking for a life change.
Frequently a founder, operator or senior individual contributor — but the
segment is a *behaviour*, not a job title: **people whose problem is deciding,
not remembering.**

**Secondary and strategically important:** anyone for whom $39 was never going
to happen. Free exists so the tool reaches them, and reach is the stated goal
rather than a byproduct.

## 4. Insight

> **"I don't have too much to do. I have too much to decide."**

The list is not the problem — the list is a symptom. The exhausting part is
carrying eleven open questions and re-deciding all of them every hour. Most
tools respond by helping capture more, which adds to the pile the person is
already carrying.

**Corollary insight, which shapes the product's manners:** the days someone most
needs this are the days they are least likely to open it. Anything that punishes
absence guarantees it will not be there on those days.

## 5. Benefits

**Functional**
- Three competing methods, credited, one chosen per day
- The chosen method enforced, not drawn
- A real duration beside each priority, planned against actual
- Daily, weekly, monthly, yearly views over the person's own record
- Full search over their whole history, free
- Export and print, always available, never gated
- Works offline, on one device, with no account

**Emotional**
- **Relief that the deciding is over for today.** The central promise
- The dignity of a tool that does not grade you
- Confidence that nothing written will be read by anyone
- Trust that it will look the same in ten years
- Being taken seriously as an adult who does not need a streak to show up

## 6. Values and personality

**Values:** honesty about what it is · restraint · privacy as architecture ·
credit where methods came from · never punishing a person for a bad week.

**Personality:** the calm colleague who asks one useful question and then stops
talking. Competent, plain, warm without being familiar. Does not perform
enthusiasm, does not sell, does not tell you how you are doing.

**If it were an object:** a Casio F-91W. Cheap, unembarrassed, everywhere,
unchanged since 1989, respected by people who own far more expensive things.

**Three things the brand never does:** diagnose, score, or exhort.

## 7. Reason to believe

- **1918 and 1954.** The methods are older than the industry selling around them
- **Named attribution** rather than rebranding — provably rarer than it should be
- **No account exists to create.** Verifiable in about four seconds
- **Free, with nothing withheld.** The archive, search and export are not gated
- **Twenty-five automated rules** fail the build when a decision is quietly
  reversed — including the ones forbidding gamified language and clinical
  vocabulary. The restraint is enforced in CI, not merely intended
- **The single scientific claim is bounded:** Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006),
  d = 0.65, if-then planning only. Everything else says *time-tested*

## 8. Discriminator

**Every other tool helps you hold more. This one is built so you can put things
down.**

And the free decision sharpens it into something no funded competitor can copy
without changing their business:

> **Three methods that predate the software industry, enforced rather than
> drawn, on a page that never leaves your device — free, because the point is
> that everyone has it.**

A subscription competitor cannot match that, because their cost to serve is
real and their changelog is their marketing.

## 9. Essence

> ### One clear day.

Three words. Everything above is the argument for them.

**Approved expressions** (`VISION.md` §11.1 holds the canonical set):
*Choose what deserves your attention.* · *Open it confused. Close it decided.* ·
*Three methods. One clear order.* · *You don't need another list. You need to
know which three.*

## Assumptions, marked as such

Recorded so nobody later mistakes them for findings:

- The target description is **inferred** from the product's design and founder
  statements. **No user research has been done. There are no users.**
- The competitive vulnerabilities are reasoning from public positioning, not
  from win/loss data.
- The emotional benefits are intended, not measured. The first real test is the
  instrumentation specified in `TELEMETRY_SPEC.md`.

**Anything here can be overturned by the first fifty real users.** It is written
to be argued with, not preserved.
