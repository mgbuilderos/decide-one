## The roof — purpose

**To give one person one clear day.**

Not a system. Not a methodology. Not a transformed life. One day, decided
rather than reacted to — and then the same again tomorrow, if they come back.

The founder's statement of it, which is the standard:

> *"If we can make one day productive for one person, that is a win for me and
> the entire team and the tool."*

This is deliberately the smallest possible ambition, and it is why the product
can be given away. A company that needs to change your life has to keep selling
you something. A tool that needs to fix today has already finished its job by
lunchtime.

---

## The vision

**That deciding what matters today stops being a thing people need software
sold to them for.**

Free, on every device, assumed rather than aspired to. The ambition is
ubiquity, not share of a premium market — a tool people keep for a decade and
never think to mention, the way nobody mentions the pen they use.

---

## The mission

**To make the deciding structured, the timing honest, and the record private —
and then to get out of the way.**

Three verbs, in order. Structure the choice. Give it a real duration. Keep what
was written on the person's own machine. Nothing after that is the product's
business.

## The five pillars

Everything the brand says stands on one of these. Copy that stands on none of
them is decoration.

### 1. Methods older than the software industry

Three, and only three: **Top 3**, **Ivy Lee** (Ivy Lee → Charles Schwab,
Bethlehem Steel, 1918), and the **Urgent/Important Matrix** (attributed to a
1954 Eisenhower speech; the matrix built by Covey). Each treats a different
failure — overcommitment, context-switching, urgency mistaken for importance.

They are named and credited, never rebranded. Borrowing a method and renaming
it is what every other tool does; saying where it came from is cheaper and more
trustworthy.

**MoSCoW, 1-3-5 and Pareto were cut and stay cut.** QC Rule 11 fails the build
if they return.

### 2. The methods are enforced, not drawn

A page can be ignored. This one will not let Ivy Lee's second line open until
the first is closed, and will not accept an urgent/important item before it is
classified.

**A clock is what makes it real.** Each line takes a duration. Three tasks at
three hours is nine hours, and the day does not have nine hours — that is found
out while planning, not at midnight.

### 3. Nothing leaves the device

No account. No password. No inbox to check. No sync, no cloud, no server
holding what someone wrote about their own day.

This is architecture, not policy — there is no server to change its mind later.
It is also why the product can be free: no marginal cost to serve, and nothing
to monetise except the thing itself.

### 4. It never scolds

Overrun adds time and says nothing. There are no streaks, no scores, no
verdicts. **A date is never treated as a verdict** — an unfinished task on a
past day is unfinished, not failed, and the software does not decide otherwise
on the person's behalf (`VISION.md` §11.3, fourth rule).

Someone returning after a month away finds a clean day, not an account of their
absence.

### 5. It is an instrument, and it is held to that standard

**The pilot flies. The compass does not.** The product's job is to tell someone
where they are so they can act — not to act for them, and not to keep them
company.

An instrument is trusted because it is **right**, not because it is pleasant. A
compass two degrees off is worse than no compass, because it is believed. So:
exact alignment, correct numbers, an accurate clock, readable type, every state
designed, zero console errors, and nothing on screen that is not information.

`VISION.md` §13 holds the full standard and the checkable list. **Ornament is a
defect** — on an instrument panel, anything that is not information is in the
way of information.

## The brand model — a Casio F-91W, not a Leica

> **Read `VISION.md` §12.1a before using this section.** Casio and Leica here are
> **belief systems, not design languages.** The point is not that the product
> should look cheap — it is that a millionaire still wears an F-91W because it
> tells the time perfectly, forever, and asks nothing of them. Function that good
> outranks aspiration.
>
> **"Premium" is not forbidden. Fluff is.** Precision, typography and durability
> are the Casio position. Decoration that signals expense while adding nothing is
> not.
>
> **The standing test:** *would someone who could afford anything still choose
> this, because it does the one job better than anything else does?*

Chosen deliberately over the premium position, and it governs price, craft,
feature policy and the name.

- **Unchanged since 1989** → the design is finished, then not touched
- **No feature ever added** → not "add slowly". Add nothing
- **Cheap and unembarrassed** → dignity from honesty about what it costs to make
- **Assumed, not aspired to** → ubiquity is the moat; exclusivity is not part of it
- **Its name is a part number** and it became beloved anyway → beauty is carried
  by behaviour, not the wordmark

**The promise this makes available, which competitors structurally cannot make:**

> This will look the same in ten years. No features will be added.

Every rival's changelog is their marketing. This one's stillness is.

**BR8 / §12.4 — now answerable, though still formally open.** The Three.js
scene, studio renders, spine curvature and woven tag were logged as *Leica
register*. §12.1a supplies the test that was missing: craft that makes the
product more precise, more durable or easier to understand is on-brand at any
level; craft whose job is to signal expense is fluff. Judge each piece against
that rather than against a style label. Until the founder rules on the specific
pieces, copy still should not describe craft as proof of quality - the product
doing its job is the proof.

**Resolved 14 September 2026, toward the instrument register** (`DECISIONS.md`
BR8). The notebook cover and the month illustrations were retired that day; the
woven tag, chassis and page curl are listed for retirement. Copy still never
describes craft as proof of quality.

## Why it is free

Not a growth tactic and not a discount. Three reasons, in order:

1. **Ubiquity is the moat.** A tool people are assumed to have beats a tool
   people aspire to buy, and that was decided before the price was.
2. **There is nothing to price against.** The price moved six times on
   11 September because no one had been asked to pay and there was no usage data
   to check any number against. Usage comes first; a number, if ever, comes from
   what usage shows.
3. **It costs nothing to run.** No server, no storage per person. A free user
   costs nothing, so reach is affordable in a way it is not for hosted software.

**The ask, when it comes:** once, at the end of a day the person actually
closed, never blocking, quieter each time it is declined. Declining is a
complete answer.

## What this brand is not

Stated because each was considered and rejected, and each will be proposed again.

| Not | Why |
| :--- | :--- |
| A habit tracker | Removed everywhere (B-11). Streaks are the punitive mechanic this product exists without |
| A second brain | The opposite ambition. This is for one day, not a lifetime of capture |
| A wellness or therapy product | §11.3 forbids clinical vocabulary outright — regulatory exposure, and it is not true |
| A productivity system with a guru | The methods are credited to the people who made them, in 1918 and 1954 |
| A luxury object performing restraint | That is Muji, Leica, a Hermès notebook. This earns respect by working, not by costing (§12.1a) |
| Freemium with a wall | Nothing is held back. Free is the product, not a trial of it |
