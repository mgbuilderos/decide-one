# Naming Direction — Familiar Authority

> Working naming brief. September 9, 2026. This document records the founder's current standard and supersedes earlier searches for beautiful, obscure, or abstract words. It does not constitute trademark clearance.

---

## 1. The standard

The name should be a word people already know, with enough existing meaning that it lends authority to the product immediately.

The founder's example is **Submarine**: it sounds substantial and technically accomplished, but it does not ask anyone to learn a new word. The name brings a whole world of engineering, purpose and capability with it.

The name does not have to be a physical object. It may be an instrument, operating principle, command term or technical concept. It must satisfy the same test:

1. **Known** — familiar on first contact; easy to say, hear and spell.
2. **Substantial** — carries weight, competence and a sense of considered construction.
3. **Relevant** — can honestly explain a tool that helps people determine what deserves their attention.
4. **Active** — suggests orientation, detection, selection, direction or commitment.
5. **Human-controlled** — the instrument helps; the person decides.
6. **Memorable** — one clear word and one dominant image or idea.

**Operative rule:** borrow authority from a familiar system, instrument or concept; do not manufacture authority with unusual vocabulary.

**Directional test:** does the borrowed meaning add to what the user sees, or take away from it? The product is a subtraction mechanism. A valid name must narrow, filter, edit, cut, rank or constrain the field rather than merely detect or display it.

---

## 2. The product meaning the name must carry

The product is a **decision instrument** for a contested day.

The user arrives with too much in their head. They identify the form of the problem, use the framework suited to it, decide what deserves the day, and commit time to it. The software supplies structure and enforcement. It never makes the judgment for them.

The naming territory can therefore draw from four functions:

| Function | What the product does | Suitable name territory |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Detect** | Separates what matters from surrounding noise | Radar, Signal |
| **Orient** | Establishes direction when the field is unclear | Vector, Bearing, Compass |
| **Operate** | Gives the user a controlled method | Protocol, Dial, Control |
| **Commit** | Holds the chosen course once the decision is made | Lock, Command, Resolve |

Names are candidates only. Several otherwise strong words have direct commercial conflicts; §4 records the current screen.

## 3. Current shortlist

### 3.1 Radar — strongest overall direction

**Why it fits:** Radar is a recognised technical instrument for finding meaningful objects within a field of noise. It does not choose the destination or make the decision; it makes the situation legible to the operator. That is very close to the product's role.

**Brand transfer:** precision, awareness, range, reliability, trained judgment.

**Natural language:**

- Open Radar.
- What is on your radar?
- Find what matters now.
- Clear the noise.

**Risk:** the word is widely used across software and other industries. Category, trademark and domain clearance remain open.

### 3.2 Protocol — strongest non-object candidate

**Why it fits:** The product asks what is going wrong and applies the established method suited to that condition. A protocol is a defined way of proceeding under known circumstances. The frameworks are enforced rules rather than decorative templates, so the word describes real product behaviour.

**Brand transfer:** expertise, rigor, repeatability, trust, correct procedure.

**Natural language:**

- Start the day's protocol.
- A protocol for deciding what deserves your day.
- Three time-tested methods. One clear decision.

**Risk:** colder than Radar and heavily used in technology, medicine and finance. Shipped copy must avoid clinical claims even if the name survives clearance.

### 3.3 Vector — strongest direction candidate

**Why it fits:** A vector expresses direction and magnitude. The product turns an undifferentiated workload into directed effort, then gives that effort a duration through the clock.

**Brand transfer:** engineering, motion, precision, purposeful direction.

**Natural language:**

- Set the day's vector.
- Give your effort a direction.
- Decide where the day goes.

**Risk:** less universally understood than Radar and widely used by technology companies.

### 3.4 Dial — strongest control candidate

**Why it fits:** A dial lets a person select a setting and read a precise state. The user identifies the challenge, switches to the corresponding framework, sets time and operates the day. It also belongs naturally beside the analogue clock.

**Brand transfer:** control, calibration, tactility, precision.

**Natural language:**

- Dial in the day.
- Set what matters.
- Choose the method. Set the time.

**Risk:** the metaphor describes operation more strongly than prioritisation.

### 3.5 Command

**Why it fits:** The product is for people with responsibility and gives them command of a contested day. It also works as both human authority and a precise instruction given to a system.

**Brand transfer:** agency, leadership, decisiveness, technical control.

**Natural language:**

- Take command of the day.
- Decide what gets your attention.

**Risk:** may feel forceful or militaristic, and can imply that the software issues the command rather than the user.

### 3.6 Mission

**Why it fits:** A mission gives many possible actions a single governing purpose. It is widely understood and inherits authority from aviation, spaceflight and disciplined operations.

**Brand transfer:** purpose, seriousness, direction, completion.

**Natural language:**

- Set today's mission.
- Decide what the day is for.

**Risk:** describes the chosen purpose more than the instrument used to find it.

### 3.7 Core

**Why it fits:** The product removes the surrounding mass until the essential work remains. Core is familiar, short and meaningful in engineering, computing and ordinary speech.

**Brand transfer:** centrality, strength, essentials, technical foundations.

**Natural language:**

- Find the core of the day.
- Keep the essential. Cut the rest.

**Risk:** extremely crowded and less active than Radar or Protocol.

### 3.8 Lock

**Why it fits:** The framework does not merely display priorities; it holds the user's decision. Three means three. Ivy Lee keeps the next item closed until the current one is complete. Lock names that commitment layer directly.

**Brand transfer:** security, firmness, precision, reliability.

**Natural language:**

- Lock the day's priorities.
- Decide once. Hold the course.

**Risk:** security and restriction are its dominant associations, so it under-represents the earlier act of deciding.

## 5. Superseded recommendation

The recommendation below was superseded after the directional test exposed a conceptual error:

- **Radar** makes the unclear field visible.
- **Protocol** applies the right method to the problem.
- **Vector** turns competing demands into directed effort.

The phone test should use complete positioning, not the word alone:

> **Radar is a decision instrument that helps you find what matters now.**

> **Protocol gives you the time-tested method for the problem your day is facing.**

> **Vector helps you decide where your effort goes today.**

Do not rename the product in code until one candidate passes verbal testing and professional trademark clearance in the relevant software classes.

---

## 6. Why Radar is rejected

A radar exists so nothing in its field escapes notice. The product exists so almost everything can leave the user's attention. The idiom *keep it on the radar* means adding another item to the set being monitored—the twenty things—while the product must cut that set down to the few that deserve the day.

Radar is therefore a well-formed name for a vigilance or coverage product. It names maximal awareness, which is the user's problem, rather than subtraction, which is the cure. Multiple live software registrations supplied by the founder independently make it commercially unsuitable.

This gives every future candidate a sharper test:

> **Does the name itself do the work?**

## 7. Active names that do the work

### Edit — strongest conceptual candidate

An editor begins with too much, exercises judgment, and removes what does not serve the purpose. The human remains the author. The product provides the editorial discipline. **Edit the day** describes the complete action with three familiar words.

Authority inherited: judgment, taste, restraint, professional craft.

### Select

Select means identifying the best or most suitable members of a larger set. It is familiar, active and technically established through controls, databases and engineering systems. Its weakness is that it can sound like an ordinary interface instruction, and the vision prefers the finality of deciding over the neutrality of choosing.

Authority inherited: criteria, precision, curation, deliberate choice.

### Refine

Refining removes impurities until the valuable material remains. It combines subtraction with improvement and carries technical, industrial and craft authority. Its weakness is that the process sounds iterative while this product asks for a firm daily decision.

Authority inherited: purity, standards, improvement, skilled process.

### Reduce

Reduce states the mechanism without metaphor. In mathematics and software it turns a collection into a smaller result; in ordinary language it removes excess. Its weakness is emotional: reduction can imply diminished ambition rather than concentrated effort.

Authority inherited: mathematics, engineering, economy, disciplined simplification.

### Shortlist

Shortlist names the concrete result: the few items that remain after evaluation. It belongs naturally to executive hiring, awards and professional selection. Its weakness is that it names the output more than the instrument, and an earlier productivity app has used the name.

Authority inherited: professional judgment, qualification, a final field.

### Current order

1. **Edit** — best match between human judgment and removal.
2. **Refine** — strongest technical and premium transfer.
3. **Select** — clearest act of taking a few from many.
4. **Reduce** — purest statement of the mechanism.
5. **Shortlist** — clearest statement of the result, but commercially weakened.

None is legally cleared. The next naming round should attempt to kill **Edit** and **Refine** first, because they are the strongest conceptual survivors.

## 8. The aspiration belongs to the operator

The founder added a necessary qualification: **Select** and **Shortlist** explain the work, but the name should also elevate the person using the instrument. The product gives the user a capability—judgment, command and decisiveness—rather than merely producing a smaller list.

The name does not need to carry the whole explanation alone. A three-part naming architecture can divide the work cleanly:

> **SELECT**  
> *A decision instrument*  
> Decide what deserves your day.

- **Select** names the action and the standard: to select, and *a select few*.
- **Decision instrument** explains what kind of product it is.
- **Decide what deserves your day** states the benefit in the user's voice.

This makes **Select** more aspirational than **Edit**. Edit foregrounds correction and removal. Select foregrounds discernment: the ability to recognise what is worthy of inclusion. The tool supplies the method; the user develops and exercises the judgment.

### Aspirational candidates

| Name | Capability given to the user | Strength | Weakness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Select** | Discernment—choosing the worthy few | Performs the work and also means carefully chosen or high quality | Common interface verb; ownership may be difficult |
| **Decisive** | The ability to reach and hold a decision | Directly names the transformation from confusion | An adjective and less tangible than an instrument word |
| **Command** | Authority over a contested day | Strong, executive and technically familiar | Can feel militaristic; subtraction is not inherent |
| **Standard** | A criterion worthy of being upheld | Carries quality, rigor and aspiration | Describes the benchmark more than the act; commercially crowded |
| **Measure** | The ability to judge what merits inclusion | Familiar instrument and standard of judgment | Can imply analytics or tracking rather than deciding |

### Revised order

1. **Select** — best balance of action, aspiration and familiarity.
2. **Decisive** — clearest aspirational transformation.
3. **Edit** — purest expression of human judgment through subtraction.
4. **Command** — strongest expression of user agency.
5. **Refine** — strongest expression of skilled improvement through removal.

The current working expression is:

> ## SELECT
> **A decision instrument**  
> Decide what deserves your day.

No candidate is selected or legally cleared. **Select** should now receive the first serious collision and trademark screen.

## 9. Coined and rhythmic forms

The founder opened a further route: retain a recognisable root but alter its rhythm, as names such as *Zomato* do, so the result is more memorable and potentially more ownable. The root must remain legible; invented luxury syllables that erase the purpose fail the familiarity test.

### Selector — rejected

The move from verb to noun—from an action to the instrument that enables it—was correct. **Selector** was the wrong landing point.

> **SELECTOR**  
> *The decision instrument*  
> Decide what deserves your day.

The `-or` suffix places it beside *Organizer, Planner, Tracker* and *Optimizer*: a functional descriptor naming the thing that performs the job. It has no distinct physical body in ordinary speech, reads as CSS or interface jargon to technical users, and remains descriptively weak in the product's own class.

**Verdict:** reject. Preserve the move from action to instrument; require the next noun to have a body, a history and a selective mechanism.

### The next candidate must pass four tests

1. **Body** — people can picture the thing.
2. **Work** — its real mechanism excludes, cuts, filters or constrains.
3. **Aspiration** — mastery of it says something elevated about its operator.
4. **Ownership** — it is not simply the software function with an agent suffix.

Promising conceptual objects for further screening are **Blade** (precise cutting), **Gate** (qualified passage), **Clutch** (selective engagement and performance under pressure), **Crucible** (testing and refinement), and **Rule** (a physical measure, an enforced method, and the act of ruling out). None is a recommendation or a cleared name.

An initial check already weakens **Rule**: a current *Rule of 3* focus planner occupies the exact productivity territory, while business-rule and decision-engine software heavily occupies its technical meaning. **Gate** also has a current task-management platform. These examples reinforce the ownership test.

## 10. Decide as the root

**Decide is the most accurate semantic root found.** It states exactly what the human does, appears in the canonical promise—*Open it confused. Close it decided*—and carries the product's subtraction philosophy in its origin: Latin *decidere*, to cut off.

Against the four-part test:

1. **Body** — the word itself is not an object, but its underlying action is a physical cut. A visual slash or cut can give that action a consistent body.
2. **Work** — exact. Deciding cuts away alternatives until one course remains.
3. **Aspiration** — strong. A person with responsibility wants to be decisive.
4. **Ownership** — weak in bare form. *Decide* directly describes the product's purpose and is occupied by active decision software.

The obvious invented derivatives are closed: **Decido** is used by several direct AI decision products; **Decisio** is used by multiple decision apps and platforms, including products for executives and structured decision frameworks; **Decida** is also active in decision support. Adding a Romance-language ending does not create useful distinctiveness in this category.

### Strongest carve: Decisive

**Decisive** preserves the familiar root and shifts the meaning from software function to human capability. It describes both a person able to decide and an action that settles an issue. That makes the aspiration belong to the operator.

> **DECISIVE**  
> *A decision instrument*  
> Cut the day to what matters.

Its body should come from the brand system rather than a fabricated object story: a single decisive cut through excess, expressed as a slash, crop or removed field. Its principal weakness is that it remains a dictionary adjective and may still be difficult to protect. An initial search found a current research framework called *Decisive* for guiding user decisions; full commercial and trademark clearance remains open.

### Forms rejected before testing

- **Cide** — technically contains the cutting root, but modern English associates `-cide` with killing.
- **Decis / Decise** — pronunciation and spelling become uncertain, repeating the Detent problem.
- **D/CIDE or D-SIDE** — visually clever but fails spoken recall and feels constructed.
- **Decider** — returns to the generic software agent-noun problem identified in Selector.

**Current conclusion:** keep **Decide** as the strategic root and test **Decisive** as the aspirational name. Do not force a novelty spelling merely to create the appearance of ownership.

### Coined forms considered

| Name | Construction | Verdict from initial screen |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Selecto** | Select + a rounded, rhythmic ending | **Reject:** active Indian photo-selection software uses the exact name |
| **Decido** | Decide + o; also reads as “I decide” in Spanish | **Reject:** multiple active decision products, including direct AI decision tools |
| **Priora** | Priority/prior + a | **Reject:** active custom-software company; adjacent software class |
| **Selora** | Select reduced to *sel* + a premium-sounding ending | **Reject:** several active software and app brands; meaning also becomes opaque |
| **Refino** | Refine + o | **Hold only as inspiration:** active IT-services brand, and the ending adds little meaning |

**Lesson:** adding a vowel is easy to remember but often makes the name feel like a generic startup. A derivative must add a useful identity, not merely a fashionable sound; **Selector** was later rejected because its identity is still a generic software function.

## 11. Instrument-grade architecture for Decide

The founder rejected **Decisive** and proposed a model-style construction such as **Decide-01**. This solves a different problem from word invention: it makes the product feel like a finished instrument while preserving the exact, familiar verb.

The strongest hierarchy is:

> # DECIDE
> **D-01 · DAILY DECISION INSTRUMENT**  
> Open it confused. Close it decided.

The elements have separate jobs:

- **DECIDE** is the brand and the human action.
- **D-01** is the model designation, giving the object a manufactured identity.
- **Daily Decision Instrument** defines the category.
- **Open it confused. Close it decided.** states the transformation.

Write **DECIDE D-01**, not *Decide-01*. The space creates a brand/model relationship; the hyphenated form reads like a software version or an attempt to modify the dictionary word. D-01 should appear as a small engraved or printed model mark, not dominate the wordmark.

This is coherent with the F-91W reference: D-01 can remain the permanent model identity rather than becoming a release sequence. It also permits the interface and physical metaphor to carry instrument authority without distorting the word into *Decido*, *Decisio* or another crowded pseudo-Latin form.

**What it does not solve:** adding a model number does not automatically make the bare word *Decide* distinctive or legally ownable. Search, app-store differentiation and trademark clearance must assess the complete composite mark **DECIDE D-01**. This is a strong identity direction, not a legal shortcut.
