## 1. Why this round was run, and what it was designed to fix

Sixteen prior searches had produced fifteen disqualifications. An earlier attempt to channel the agencies *inline* failed — it recycled names already on the list (Touchstone, Fewer, Enough, Three) because the same mind was asking the same question in different costumes. The founder correctly called this out.

The fix was genuine independence: separate agents, cold context, reading `VISION.md` for themselves without the anchoring of the previous rounds.

**Two design decisions did most of the work:**

1. **Each agency searched its own candidates.** Previous rounds generated beautiful names that I then disqualified one at a time. Here the generation and the killing happen in the same head.
2. **Six agencies, not one.** This turned out to matter more than expected — see §3.

## 2. What each agency produced

### Wieden+Kennedy — *the name as a stance*

Built the entire list on one reading of the brief: *decide* = to cut off, kanso = subtraction, the method is enforced not drawn. Therefore **every candidate is a tool that cuts, holds or trues** — nothing that merely displays.

**Ten:** Carve · Chisel · Latch · Wedge · Cleave · Shear · Plumb · True · Cull · Whittle

**Recommended: Plumb** — the mason's line that finds true vertical no matter how crooked the wall looks. *Gravity decides, not more thinking.* Unchanged for millennia, so it matches "the century-old method"; a real object you could photograph.
*Their stated weakness:* passive until read; a beat needed to parse the metaphor.

### Ogilvy — *the name as a promise of quality*

**Ten:** Whittle · Shears · Cleave · Chisel · Escapement · Chronometer · Baton · Gavel · Plumb · Bevel

**Recommended: Whittle** — universally picturable, a verb the brand can use ("whittle it to three"), load-bearing on Rule of 3, kanso, Laozi and *decidere* at once.
*Their stated weakness:* "whittle down" is a common idiom, so the mark must work to feel owned; register leans homespun rather than luxury.

### Dentsu Tokyo — *the name as a small object*

**Ten:** Nagi · Sumi · Toki · Fude · Deckle · Bezel · Temper · Whittle · Chop · Kozo

**Recommended: Nagi (凪)** — the Japanese word for the stillness after wind or wave drops. Names the *state the product produces*, which is `VISION.md` §4 exactly. Two clean syllables, no clusters, spellable on sight; passes the Muji/Sony test.
*Their stated weakness:* no English cognate, so the tagline must carry first contact; may read as a person's name.

### BBDO — *the name as mass legibility*

**Ten:** Verdict · Gavel · Decree · Fulcrum · Distill · Whittle · Ingot · Hone · Docket · Refine

**Recommended: Verdict** — passes billboard, App Store and boardroom with no follow-up sentence; existing "Verdict" apps sit in unrelated categories, so nobody owns the productivity meaning.
*Their stated weakness, and it is serious:* **a verdict is normally passed *on* you.** `VISION.md` §11.3 rule C9 states the product observes and never judges. Their own recommendation conflicts with a standing rule, and their proposed fix ("your verdict," never "the verdict") is copy patching a structural mismatch.

### 72andSunny — *the name as something a person would say*

**Ten:** Plumb · Threshold · Whetstone · Keel · First Thing · Muster · Vow · Kindling · Foothold · Threefold

**Recommended: Plumb** — the plumb bob is one of the oldest tools in human use, unchanged for millennia, which lands the "older than the software industry" claim without stating it. Carries *plumb* as in exactly/truly, and *to plumb* as in to get to the bottom of.
*Their stated weakness, and it is decisive:* **the homophone with Plum**, a well-known UK money-management app. *"Try Plumb" and "try Plum" are indistinguishable said aloud* — fatal in a category built on word of mouth.

**Their kills:** **Keel** — the worst find of the round: **"Keel: Your Focus Anchor," a live AI daily planner that builds your plan and tracks your priorities.** Effectively this product, shipping. Also **Vow** (wedding-tech has saturated it — Vow AI Wedding Planner, VowFlow, Vowen, VowsVibe, Vow Timer), **Muster** (a $30M-revenue B2B software company, *independently confirming Droga5*), **Kindling** (five apps including a habit tracker), **Threshold** (Target Brands holds a heavily-registered mark; different class, but crowded).

### Droga5 — *the name as a story*

**Ten:** Caesura · Cleave · Stet · Tzimtzum · Assay · Cull · Decidere · Thirty-Nine · Foolscap · Neti

**Recommended: Decidere** — the Latin the hero line already runs on, *de* + *caedere*, to cut off. Their argument is the sharpest of the six: **you do not bolt an etymology onto this name; the name *is* the etymology.** No story to explain, because the product's own copy already tells it.

## 6A. The round's most valuable insight — tool words versus state words

72andSunny was the only agency explicitly briefed on the founder's tension: the name must be as unpretentious as "Clearing" but as dignified as an instrument. Their answer explains the entire history of this search:

> *"A plain, warm word **can** feel elevated — but not by leaning into abstract states like Clearing, Steady, or Enough. Those describe a **feeling**, and feelings-as-names read as generic wellness-app filler. It works when the plain word is a **physical instrument with real history** — something with weight, a shape, a use that predates software. Plumb and Whetstone earn aspiration through **craft**, not vocabulary.*
> ***The founder's instinct that plain words feel basic was correct for state words. It's wrong for tool words.***"

**This resolves the objection that reopened the search.** Clearing, Steady, Margin, Enough and Nagi are all **state words** — they name a feeling, and they all read thin. Shears, Whetstone, Plumb, Chisel and Escapement are **tool words** — they earn elevation through craft rather than through vocabulary.

It also explains §4's convergence: five independent agencies reached for instruments because that is the class that carries altitude without needing Latin. And it is a live critique of Dentsu's **Nagi**, which names the stillness after the wind drops — a state, not a tool.

**Operative rule going forward: prefer tool words. Distrust state words.**

## 9. The scientist-eponym round — September 9–10, 2026

The founder proposed naming the product after a physical principle rather than an object. **Standing constraint: no invented quotations.** Each candidate states the principle accurately and builds original copy from it. Nothing is attributed to any scientist as speech or opinion.

### 9.1 SCHMITT D1

**Principle.** Otto H. Schmitt built the circuit in 1934 as a graduate student studying nerve impulse propagation, publishing it in 1938 as *A Thermionic Trigger*. It is a comparator with **hysteresis** — two thresholds rather than one. Output flips when input passes the upper threshold and will not flip back until input falls below a lower one. The gap is the deadband. A noisy input therefore produces one clean output that does not chatter.

**Two assets:** *conversion* (continuous mess → one discrete state) and *hysteresis* (the state holds; only a real change reverses it). The second is unclaimed in this category and is the deeper product truth — a single-threshold to-do list is a comparator without hysteresis, and it chatters all day.

**Founder's line, retained:** *Turn noise into a decision.* Imperative and human-agent, which satisfies `NAMING_DIRECTION.md` Rule 5 better than the colder instrument-voice alternatives.

**Second tier:** *One threshold to decide. Another to change your mind.*

**Directional test (§1):** passes. Hysteresis is a refusal mechanism; it discards everything below threshold. The opposite of Radar.

### 9.2 FERMI K1 — rejected

**Principle.** *k* is the neutron multiplication factor. k<1 subcritical, the reaction dies; k>1 supercritical, it grows each generation; k=1 critical, exactly self-sustaining. Chicago Pile-1 reached and held k=1 on 2 December 1942, controlled by cadmium rods moved by hand.

**Rejected on four grounds, in severity order:**

1. **Wrong device class.** A Schmitt trigger is an *edge* device; a reactor at k=1 is an *equilibrium* device. This product is a daily threshold crossing, not a steady state. Fermi describes maintenance where the product sells commitment.
2. **The loud meaning beats the narrow one.** The audience must hold "control" while recall supplies "chain reaction." This is the §1 directional failure that killed Radar — the borrowed meaning adds energy rather than subtracting field.
3. **Occupancy, semantically adjacent.** NVIDIA's Fermi GPU architecture, Fermilab, the Fermi Gamma-ray Space Telescope, the Fermi paradox — and worst, **Fermi estimation**, already native to this audience's productivity vocabulary.
4. **Permanent Manhattan Project shadow.** Every explanation of the name walks past it. "Critical" in ordinary English also means *in danger*.

### 9.3 PAULI — rejected, and it produced the round's finding

**Principle.** Pauli established in 1925 that no two identical fermions may occupy the same quantum state. Correctly stated, exclusion applies to **every** state, not the ground state — one state, one occupant, all the way up. Electrons therefore fill successive shells; matter has extent and resists compression.

**Two assets, both retained as doctrine regardless of the wordmark:**

- **The whole ordering is exclusive.** This is not an analogy to Rule of 3 and Ivy Lee; it is a description of them. Ivy Lee is six states, one occupant each, worked in order.
- **Exclusion displaces, it does not delete.** The electron that cannot occupy 1s is pushed to 2s, intact. Every competitor's metaphor for deprioritising is a graveyard — archive, backlog, someday. This answers the anxiety that actually prevents prioritising: *if I don't do it today it dies.*

**The founder's convergence, retained:** the 1925 physics and the etymology make the same claim from unrelated authorities — nothing shares a state; *priority* had no plural for five centuries. **Caveat:** the etymological claim is a popularised framing (McKeown, *Essentialism*). Broadly supported by the record; usable as a claim about English, never as a citation.

**Rejected.** The founder's own diagnosis on Sept 10: the exclusion-principle story does all the work, and users who never meet the story remember the name without understanding it.

### 9.4 §6A extension — eponyms are a third class

§6A distinguishes **tool words** (elevate through craft) from **state words** (name a feeling, read thin). PAULI is neither.

> **An eponym has no shape. It has a citation.** You can hold shears, a plumb, a whetstone. You cannot hold a Pauli.

This is the **concreteness defect a third time** — Merit (N4), Primacy (score 19), now Pauli. The pattern is the finding.

**Why the round happened at all:** N7 established the good familiar words are gone and N8 that the empty register is machine parts. Eponyms looked like fresh territory. They are — but they trade an occupancy problem for a meaning problem.

**Operative rule added: distrust eponyms. They must be taught by lecture; mechanisms teach by use.** Every time a user feels the product hold a decision, a mechanism name is re-explained. A citation can only ever be re-told.

**On the Tesla comparison.** The mechanism is real but the analogy flatters the candidate: Tesla had billions in earned media, a physical object on every street teaching the name for free, and popular recognition of the eponym ("Tesla = electricity") far broader than "Pauli = exclusion principle." It is also survivorship bias — hundreds of taught-name startups took the same bet unremembered.

**Trademark note (not legal advice, and not clearance).** Under Lanham Act §2(e)(4) the USPTO refuses marks that are "primarily merely a surname" absent acquired distinctiveness. PAULI has a plausible *Benthin* escape — rare US surname, nobody at the company bears it, other recognised meanings — but **the brand story and the trademark argument pull in opposite directions**: leaning on "named after the physicist" supplies the examiner's evidence. EU has no surname bar; India's s.14 does not apply (Pauli died 1958, beyond the 20-year window). Publicity rights are a non-issue. Largest commercial user of the string in Europe is **FC St. Pauli**, which enforces its brand — different classes, likely survivable, must be checked. **Registrability and semantic emptiness share one root: the name is primarily a surname.**

## 10. The Detent convergence — and why it does not reopen the question

> ⚠️ **Correction, recorded after the fact.** This section was written before `DECISIONS.md` **N13** was re-read. N13 had already superseded Detent — *"it met the machine-part brief but failed the familiarity and spoken-word tests"* (`VISION.md` §12.6). The analysis below independently rediscovered those same two flaws, which **confirms N13 rather than challenging it. Detent is not reopened.** The section is retained because three findings survive under any name: the mechanism-to-product mapping, the *lock versus detent* category position (§10, table and note), and the interaction spec in the paragraph beginning "It writes the interaction spec." The standing direction remains N19: **DECIDE · D-01 · Daily Decision Instrument.**

`DECISIONS.md` N9 found the tool word that carries both physics candidates; N13 later set it aside.

| Detent property | Product behaviour |
| :--- | :--- |
| Discrete notches, not a continuous track | Every shipped framework is a set of slots. Rule of 3 is three notches; Ivy Lee six, worked in order; Eisenhower four cells. **The frameworks are already detented** — none is a slider |
| One occupant per notch | Nothing shares first — Pauli's exclusion, stated mechanically instead of by citation |
| Unstable between notches | The dial will not rest between positions. Open ritual forces a landing; day-closure forces a resolution. No limbo |
| Deliberate force to move | Schmitt's hysteresis with a body. Things that merely *arrive* do not reshuffle the three |
| Never locked — no key, no permission | Rule 5 exactly. It resists; it never refuses |
| Passive: no power, no state, no authority | Local-first, offline, no account, one payment |
| The click | Commitment should snap, not fade |
| The spring is the resistance | The cap *is* the spring — Rule of 3 pushing back on a fourth item |

**Essence:** a detent makes drift expensive and decision cheap.

**Category position this hands us.** Freedom, Cold Turkey, Forest and one-sec ship **locks** — block, kill, guilt, withhold. Their mechanism is coercion and requires pre-commitment against one's own future judgment. A detent costs something to move and never once stops you. *Not the app that locks the door — the notch that holds the dial.*

**It writes the interaction spec.** Adopting it makes three things obligatory: changing the day's items must take a deliberate action **with resistance** (nothing in the code enforces this today); committing must produce **discrete tactile confirmation**, not a toast or a fade; the cap must **push back visibly** at a fourth item.

**Etymology, which resolves the N9 flaw.** From Old French *détente*, the release catch of a crossbow, from *détendre*, to slacken (Latin *tendere*). The diplomatic *détente* is the same word on a different branch. The word originally named the part that *releases* and English shifted it to the part that *holds*. Pronounced /dɪˈTENT/ against /day-TAHNT/ — **less confusable spoken than written**, so N9's flaw is a reading problem more than a hearing one.

**Honest flaws.** Recognition among software founders and executives is low — perhaps one in ten. It **fails N12's *Submarine* standard** on "widely known" and partly on "easy to say." Search results skew hard to AR-15 parts suppliers. And per `NAMING_DIRECTION.md` §2 it covers only **Operate and Commit** — it is silent on **Detect and Orient**, i.e. the diagnostic step, arguably the product's most distinctive feature. Radar covered Detect and could not subtract; Detent subtracts and cannot diagnose.

**Mitigation that PAULI lacks:** teaching cost is one sentence with a universal referent — *"the click-stops on a camera dial."* Every buyer has felt a detent today. The gap is vocabulary, not experience.

### 10.1 The spoken-referral test — the one that decides it

Say the sentence that grows an indie product: **"You should try ___."**

- *"You should try Pauli."* → written down as "Pauly." Easy to say, carries nothing.
- *"You should try Detent."* → written down as "détente." Carries everything, hard to transmit.

**Both fail, in opposite directions.** This is not aesthetic, it is **distributional** — it breaks the cheapest channel available. N9 half-caught this as "the Chronometer failure mode."

**Counterweight, recorded against the recommendation:** on sayability PAULI is stronger. Figma, Miro, Asana and Zapier are all meaningless and all trivially sayable — and all had funding to teach meaning. If word of mouth is the primary channel, sayability may outrank inherent meaning.

**Cheap experiment, extending DECISIONS step 5:** say each name to five people outside the project with no context; ask them to spell it back and guess what it does. Spelling accuracy measures survival in word of mouth; guesses measure what the name teaches alone.

> **Note on step 5's own wording.** The existing phone test is *"I keep my priorities in ___."* If the Pauli doctrine is adopted, the plural contradicts it. Use *"I keep my priority in ___"* or drop the sentence.

## 11. EMPTY HEAD — rejected

Proposed with the line *"You write priority so your head is free."* Rejected on four grounds:

1. **"Empty-headed" means stupid in English.** Airhead, vacant, dim. Unshippable against an executive buyer at any price.
2. **The idea belongs to GTD.** *"Your mind is for having ideas, not holding them"* is among the most famous lines in the category. The name would borrow a competing methodology's core premise.
3. **It describes the wrong product.** GTD's empty head means *capture everything into a trusted system.* This product's thesis is the opposite — do not capture everything, choose three. Emptying your head into a list is the inbox model already rejected, and O Space files it as a symptom: *Planning Obsession*.
4. **§6A: a state word**, naming a feeling of relief.

**Mechanism error in the line.** Writing does not free the head — *deciding* does. A list of forty items frees nothing. The relief comes from the cap, not the capture, and the line credits the wrong half of the product. It also needs an article, and "priorities" is forbidden by the product's own doctrine.

**Salvage — the benefit is real, the framing is wrong.** Not *empty* (insult, and implies nothing remains) but the consequence of exclusion: **you carry one thing at a time.**

> **Decide once. Carry one.**

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Diagnosis | **Once.** Test, report, coaching | **Daily.** Every morning, what today is |
| What it tells you | Who you are — a fixed root pillar | What today is — a changing condition |
| Frameworks | Proprietary, coach-mediated | Public domain, unrenamed, user-run |
| Business | Services, €300–€3,000 | $39 once, local, nobody in the loop |
| Brand | The founder's face and story | An instrument, deliberately impersonal |

> **They diagnose the person. We diagnose the day.** A personality test is interesting once. A question with a different answer every morning is why someone opens it every morning.

**Pricing signal:** €3,000 against $39 is roughly 77×, with services overbooked. Demand for diagnosis-led help materially exceeds supply at the top of the market. Referred to `MONETIZATION_PLAN.md` — this is at minimum evidence the product is not overpriced.

**Copy collision warning:** *"Your Productivity Isn't Broken. Your System Is."* is close to our positioning argument. Know it exists before writing launch copy.

### 12.2 Vocabulary harvest — zero usable names

Full lexicon across four pages: procrastination, overwhelm, perfectionism, decision fatigue, self-sabotage, burnout, time blindness, imposter syndrome, people pleasing, fear of failure, scattered focus, chronic urgency, overthinking, guilt cycles, misaligned priorities, energy crashes, delegation fear, context switching, emotional reactivity, comparison trap, motivation dependency, planning obsession, identity confusion, avoidance; plus pillar, root, symptom, pattern, blocker, rewire, high-signal, operating unit, shift, space.

**Every noun is a symptom label** — a name for what is wrong with the person. §6A distrusts state words for naming a feeling; these are a rung below, naming a **dysfunction**. The register is the exact inverse of Submarine, Detent, Escapement, Plumb, Shears. The only word with pull is **Signal**, which is dead as a wordmark and sits under Detect, the function that killed Radar.

**Finding: the coaching register yields no instrument names. Do not search it again.**

### 12.3 The natural experiment — an empty name, in our category, paying its tax

**"The O Space" is PAULI's failure mode, live.** Semantically empty, ambiguous spoken (*oh space? zero space?*), unsearchable. Observe the compensation: the founder's face, name, burnout story, credentials and case count pushed to the front. **The personal brand carries the name because the name carries nothing.**

She can pay that tax — she is a coach, the person *is* the product, and 500 clients came through her rather than through the word. **We cannot.** The F-91W thesis is an instrument with no personality behind it. A name requiring a founder to explain it is structurally incompatible with the brand already chosen. This is the most concrete argument against PAULI in the round.

### 12.4 Adaptation to take — named conditions as the entry surface

The 24 labels demonstrate that people **readily self-identify with a named condition**. *"Everything feels urgent"* is a far better front door than a dropdown reading *Select framework*.

**Adopt:** the morning question presents a short list of plain-language conditions in the user's own voice and routes silently to the framework. The user need never learn that Eisenhower exists until after it has helped them. Costs nothing, improves the diagnostic step, and keeps the frameworks unrenamed per M-A7.

**Do not adopt:** the pathology framing. Their list names 24 things wrong with *you*. Ours must name conditions of the *day* — a day can be overloaded without the person being defective. This distinction is the whole difference in register between a coaching brand and an instrument brand.

## 14. The cross-linguistic finding — doctrine, not naming

Run September 10, 2026, after the eponym round closed. It is the strongest doctrinal material this project has produced, and unlike Schmitt or Pauli **it requires no teaching** — the reader half-knows it before you say it.

### 14.1 "Decide" is built from "cut" in language after language

| Language | Word for *decide* | Literal sense |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Latin | **decidere** | *de* + *caedere* — **to cut off** (the root of English *decide*) |
| French | **trancher** | **to slice through** — used idiomatically for settling a question |
| German | **entscheiden** | ← *scheiden*, **to separate** |
| Japanese | **ketsudan** 決断 | decide + **sever** |
| Chinese | **juéduàn** 决断 | the same two characters |
| Arabic | **faṣl** فصل · **ḥasm** حسم | **separation** · **cutting off** |
| Greek | **krinein** κρίνειν | **to separate** → *krisis*, *criterion*, *critic* |
| Sanskrit | **viveka** | **discrimination**, separating one from another |
| Finnish | **päättää** | to decide **and to end** (← *pää*, head/end) |

**Five unrelated language families independently built the word for deciding out of cutting or separating.** Not choosing. Not adding. Not planning.

This is why `DECIDE ONE` satisfies §1's directional test **by the word's own meaning** rather than by an argument that has to be made (N30).

> **Every language agrees: to decide is to cut.**

### 14.2 "Priority" is never about cutting — it is about position

Latin *prior* (earlier) · German *Vorrang* (fore-rank) · Finnish *etusija* (front seat) · Hebrew *rishon* (← *rosh*, head) · Japanese *yūsen* (ahead) · Sanskrit *prathama* (first) · Arabic *awwal* (first). Every one is **positional**.

| | Means | Answers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Priority** | Position in a line | *What goes first?* |
| **Decide** | Cutting away | *What doesn't go at all?* |

**This maps onto §1B's two-layer structure exactly:** the **recto** is decision — the cut, what never reaches the page. The **verso** is priority — position, sequence, what runs first. The name carries the recto; the descriptor *Priority instrument* carries the verso.

### 14.3 Right of way — a third framing, unclaimed

German **Vorfahrt**, Dutch **voorrang** and Swedish **företräde** each mean *priority* **and** *right of way*. Those languages treat them as one concept: **who goes first when two things arrive at the same moment.**

That is the product, with genuine familiar authority behind it — every driver knows right of way, nobody must be taught it, and it is a rule that decides between competing claims **without deciding for you** (Rule 5).

> **Two things arrive at once. Something has to have right of way.**

Usable in copy today. Not proposed as a name — the English forms are long and *Yield* belongs to finance.

### 14.4 Standing caveat on etymology

The *"priority had no plural for five hundred years"* line is a **popularised framing** (McKeown, *Essentialism*), broadly supported by the record. Usable as a claim about English; **never as a citation.** The cut-metaphor table above is ordinary lexicography and does not carry that caveat.

| **Deliberate misspellings** | Decyde, Pryority, Dcide | **Trademark law examines sound** — phonetically identical marks conflict, so a respelling escapes a *domain*, not a *register*. Both are laudatory and routinely disclaimed, adding **no** distinctiveness to a near-descriptive root. `Decide Now!` is also a live app with its own domain |

**Meta-finding.** Every register above closed for one of two reasons: **the known words are taken, or the available words are unknown.** That is N7's saturation thesis reappearing in ten new costumes. It is not a failure of taste — it is the predicted behaviour of a picked-clean field, and it is why the answer was a **plain word plus a doctrine-carrying second word** rather than a more beautiful single one.

> **A useful stopping rule, learned the hard way: after nineteen names, the remaining choice is between two failure modes, and no further analysis resolves a judgment call. Only contact with a real person does.**
